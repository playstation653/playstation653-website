What open play is

Unstructured, on purpose

Nobody runs a program. Nobody blows a whistle. Children move between nine pretend-play stations at whatever pace makes sense to them, which is exactly how small children learn best.

Open play is where a two-year-old spends forty minutes putting the same block in the same bucket, and where a five-year-old runs a pretend diner with three strangers she has just decided are her staff. Both are fine. Both are the point.

Caregivers stay with their children, which means you get to see it happen — and the café tables mean you can do it sitting down with a coffee.

House rules

Five things, and then we're done

1

No reservation needed — walk in any time during open play hours.

2

A signed waiver is required before entry. Sign it online to skip the line.

3

Shoes off, socks on — grippy socks for everyone, kids and grown-ups.

4

Every child must be accompanied by an adult at all times. This is not a drop-off center.

5

Outside food and drink are welcome in the café area. No food in the play space.

For the grown-ups

There is a chair with your name on it

A café area with real seating, counter stools and tables, positioned so you can see the whole room.

Bring your own coffee and snacks — outside food and drink are welcome at the café tables. There's a changing station in the restroom and free parking directly out front of the side entrance on Ridgeway Drive.

Two adults per family are always included. Additional adults are $5, because grandparents come in packs.
